Health Brain

Privacy policy

Effective September 13, 2026

Who operates Health Brain

Health Brain is a private personal journal operated by Taylor Perry. This policy covers the journal and its optional Google Health connection. Questions or requests about data can be sent to yrreprolyat@gmail.com.

Information accessed and collected

The journal stores submitted workout details, bodyweight, notes, voice transcripts, capture times and timezones, and the history of commands and corrections. When configured, the Voltra connection also stores raw exercise telemetry and derived set records.

After explicit Google authorization, Health Brain reads sleep records, exercise sessions, daily heart rate variability, and daily resting heart rate through the Google Health API. The requested permissions are sleep, health metrics and measurements, and activity and fitness, all read-only. The app stores returned records, their revisions, and retrieval information. It does not request Google location, profile, or write permissions.

Google authorization credentials and tokens are stored separately on the owner's server so the application can refresh access and import data without requiring a new sign-in for each import. The private application also uses the authorized network identity to restrict access.

How information is used and shared

Journal entries and imported observations are used to display personal history, compare workouts, show health trends, and support corrections and recovery from interrupted imports. Speech recognition and command interpretation run on the owner's server.

Health Brain does not sell personal data, use it for advertising, or disclose journal contents to third-party analytics services. Google Health records are not used to train machine-learning models or sent to external AI services. Requests to Google carry the credentials and query parameters needed to retrieve the authorized records; journal entries are not uploaded to Google Health.

Google data is used only to provide the personal health history features described here. Health Brain's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Storage and access

The application database, imported source records, audit history, and backups are stored on the owner's server. The journal is restricted to the owner's private network and authorized identity. Authorization files have restricted filesystem permissions.

The phone browser can retain unsynced text and recordings for later upload and up to 20 recent submission receipts, which may include transcripts. Queued audio is removed after the server acknowledges it. Uploaded recordings are decoded in memory and are not retained as server audio files; their transcripts may remain in the journal. A configured local voice listener retains pending transcripts for retry rather than continuously saving audio.

These public information pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. They contain no journal data, sign-in form, analytics script, or advertising. The website host may process ordinary request information such as IP addresses under GitHub's privacy statement.

Retention, revocation, and deletion

Journal records, original imports, corrections, and backups are retained until the owner removes them. An edit or undo changes the current journal view but does not erase the underlying audit history. There is no automatic expiration period for server records.

Google access can be revoked through Google Account connections. Revocation stops future authorized imports; it does not automatically erase information already stored by Health Brain.

To delete imported data or other journal records, the owner can contact the operator at the address above to remove the relevant database records, retained source files, authorization files, and backups. Deletion is a manual administrative process, not an in-app button. Browser site data must also be cleared on devices that hold local captures or receipts; doing so removes unsynced entries.

Changes

This page will be updated when the application's data practices change. The effective date identifies the current version.
